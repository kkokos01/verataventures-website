# Verata Ventures Brand Guide

Version 1.1 · 22 July 2026

## 1. The brand in one page

### Brand idea

**Judgment, accelerated.**

Verata Ventures combines the judgment, accountability, and organizational fluency
of an experienced executive with the speed and leverage of an AI-native venture
builder.

### The desired impression

Verata should feel like a highly capable operating partner who can enter a Fortune
50 boardroom, a venture investment discussion, or a founder working session without
changing character. The organization is founder-led and intentionally compact. Its
credibility comes from judgment and evidence, not from implying a larger team than
exists.

### Positioning statement

Verata Ventures is a founder-led venture studio that applies executive judgment and
AI-enabled execution to build, validate, and operate new businesses. It works with
enterprises, capital partners, and individual operators who value senior
accountability and startup velocity.

### Core promise

**One accountable operator. Multiplied execution.**

### Brand personality

- **Experienced, not established-looking:** credible without inherited visual baggage.
- **Fast, not frantic:** visible momentum with controlled decisions.
- **Direct, not blunt:** plain language with respect for complexity.
- **Technical, not technocratic:** comfortable with AI and systems without performing expertise.
- **Ambitious, not inflated:** consequential work without fake scale or hype.

### Visual system

- **Name:** Live Mineral / Humanist Intelligence
- **Default mode:** light
- **High-energy mode:** saturated dark
- **Public voice:** Newsreader
- **Operating voice:** Instrument Sans
- **Evidence voice:** Geist Mono
- **Primary colors:** cool mist, deep ink, mineral teal
- **Signals:** volt citron and signal coral, used sparingly
- **Layout:** executive editorial grid with operational metadata

## 2. Audience posture

Verata presents one coherent identity to three audiences. The emphasis changes, but
the character does not.

### Venture capital and investment partners

Lead with pattern recognition, capital efficiency, direct access, and the ability to
move from thesis to evidence quickly. Avoid language that implies Verata is a fund
unless that becomes factually true.

**What they should conclude:** Verata can turn a credible idea into a testable,
operating venture without wasting time or capital.

### Corporate partners

Lead with executive judgment, organizational fluency, governance, phase-gated
decisions, and the ability to work within real constraints. Avoid startup theater or
claims that speed requires ignoring controls.

**What they should conclude:** Verata understands how large organizations make
decisions and can introduce startup speed without introducing startup chaos.

### Individual founders and operators

Lead with direct senior access, pragmatic help, honesty, and high-output execution.
Avoid presenting an anonymous institution between the client and the work.

**What they should conclude:** they will work with the person whose judgment they
bought, and progress will be visible.

## 3. Messaging system

### Primary line

**Judgment, accelerated.**

Use as a brand sign-off, presentation close, metadata line, or short campaign
statement. Do not repeat it more than once per artifact.

### Primary explanatory line

**Executive judgment. Startup velocity.**

Use when the audience needs immediate context. It works well as a website headline
or presentation opener.

### Supporting statement

**Founder-led venture building for enterprises, capital, and operators.**

Use as a descriptor, not as the hero claim on every artifact.

### Short company description

Verata Ventures is a founder-led venture studio that combines experienced executive
judgment with AI-enabled execution to build and operate new businesses.

### Standard company description

Verata Ventures builds, validates, and operates new businesses for enterprises,
capital partners, and individual operators. The studio combines the judgment and
organizational fluency of an experienced executive with an AI-native operating model
designed for speed, evidence, and direct accountability.

### Proof language

Prefer concrete operating evidence over adjectives:

- phase completed
- customer signal validated
- production release shipped
- decision recommended
- capital required
- time to first evidence
- operating constraint resolved

Avoid unsupported claims such as “world-class,” “unparalleled,” “revolutionary,” or
“industry-leading.”

## 4. Voice and writing

### Voice principles

#### Lead with the decision

State the conclusion, recommendation, or value first. Context follows.

**Use:** “Proceed to the pilot. Three customer signals are strong enough to justify
the next phase.”

**Avoid:** “After a detailed evaluation of a number of potentially relevant factors,
we believe there may be an opportunity to consider moving forward.”

#### Write like an operator

Use specific nouns, active verbs, dates, owners, and status. Replace abstract claims
with observable work.

#### Show measured conviction

Make recommendations. Name uncertainty without hiding behind it. Distinguish fact,
inference, and judgment when the difference matters.

#### Preserve human authority

The prose should sound like a thoughtful senior person, not a corporate committee or
an AI assistant. Avoid filler, inflated vocabulary, and generic enthusiasm.

#### Respect the reader's time

Headlines are usually 3 to 9 words. Paragraphs are usually 2 to 4 sentences. Use
lists for decisions, evidence, requirements, and next actions.

### Point of view

- Use **Verata Ventures** when describing the company or operating model.
- Use **Kevin** or **I** for founder-authored notes, opinions, and direct outreach.
- Use **we** only when it truthfully refers to Verata plus collaborators, a product
  team, or a client team. Do not use “we” to imply organizational scale.

### Vocabulary to favor

judgment, evidence, operating, build, validate, decision, phase, signal, partner,
traction, accountable, direct, practical, specific, shipped, recommended

### Vocabulary to avoid

disrupt, revolutionize, unlock, game-changing, cutting-edge, seamless, leverage as a
verb, synergy, ecosystem when “partners” is sufficient, world-class, thought leader

### Punctuation and casing

- Use sentence case for headlines.
- Use uppercase only for short metadata labels and status.
- Prefer periods and colons to ornamental punctuation.
- Use ampersands only in established labels or compact navigation.
- Use numerals for dates, phase numbers, and operating metrics.

## 5. Logo system

**Status: locked master.** The geometry approved on 22 July 2026 is canonical.
Reuse the supplied assets; do not redraw the mark from a screenshot.

### Concept

The mark is a one-to-many V. One incoming stroke reaches the lower vertex. Three
parallel outgoing strokes rise from it at equal angles and increasing lengths. The
four upper terminals end flat on one shared horizontal line. The construction
represents one accountable operator with multiplied execution.

The incoming stroke and longest outgoing stroke meet in a shared two-color miter.
They do not use independently capped or casually overlapping rectangles. Exact
construction coordinates and asset checksums are recorded in `brand/assets/README.md`.

### Approved forms

1. **Light formal:** ink incoming stroke, three mineral outgoing strokes.
2. **Dark formal:** pale incoming stroke, three bright-mineral outgoing strokes.
3. **Dark expressive:** pale incoming stroke with mineral, citron, and coral outgoing
   strokes. Reserve this for launches, events, social assets, dark presentation
   dividers, and other high-energy moments.

### Wordmark

Set **VERATA** in Newsreader at weight 500 with generous tracking. Set **VENTURES**
in Instrument Sans uppercase at weight 500 with tracking between 0.22em and 0.28em.
Do not synthesize a wordmark in another serif or substitute the mono font.

### Clear space

Maintain clear space equal to at least half the mark width on every side. No text,
rules, images, or page edges enter this area.

### Minimum sizes

- Mark only: 24px digital or 8mm print.
- Horizontal lockup: 120px digital or 32mm print.
- Below these sizes, remove small descriptors before reducing the mark further.

### Do not

- change the parallel relationship, stroke angles, or relative lengths
- stagger, angle, round, or otherwise separate the four flat upper terminals
- replace the shared lower miter with overlapping end caps
- round the stroke terminals
- place the multicolor mark on the light background
- add shadows, glow, bevel, outline, or gradient
- rotate or crop the mark
- place it inside a pill, badge, or decorative container
- use citron or coral as the dominant logo color

Source assets are in `brand/assets/`.

### Light mode

Light mode is the everyday executive expression. Use Cool Mist for the page, Paper
for deliberate surface changes, Deep Ink for text, and Mineral Teal for roughly 5 to
8 percent of the composition. Citron and coral should usually occupy less than 2
percent combined.

### Dark mode

Dark mode is a more saturated expression, not a simple inversion. Use Deep Current
as the field, Current Surface for contained artifacts, Pale for text, and Mineral
Bright for key rules and labels. Citron and coral may become more visible, but remain
signals rather than large background fields.

### Accessibility rules

- Deep Ink on Cool Mist: 16.49:1.
- Ink Soft on Cool Mist: 8.86:1.
- Steel on Cool Mist: 4.89:1.
- Mineral Text on Cool Mist: 4.82:1.
- Pale on Deep Current: 15.72:1.
- Pale Muted on Deep Current: 9.51:1.
- Mineral Bright on Deep Current: 6.70:1.
- Citron on Deep Current: 12.81:1.
- Coral on Deep Current: 5.38:1.

Do not use Mineral Teal, citron, or coral as small text on the light background.
Citron and coral may be used as dots, rules, icons, or backgrounds with Deep Ink text.
Never rely on color alone to communicate status.

## 7. Typography

All three families are open source under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

- Newsreader source: <https://github.com/productiontype/Newsreader>
- Instrument Sans source: <https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-sans>
- Geist source and web installation: <https://vercel.com/font>

### Newsreader: public and editorial voice

Use Newsreader for website heroes, public-facing section titles, presentation covers,
editorial cards, pull quotes, and selected campaign statements. It provides human
judgment without the inherited formality of a traditional corporate serif.

- Preferred weights: 400 and 500.
- Use optical sizing when supported.
- Set large headlines tightly, between `-0.025em` and `-0.045em`.
- Keep line lengths short.
- Do not use in navigation, forms, tables, captions, or operational memos.

### Instrument Sans: operating voice

Use Instrument Sans for body copy, navigation, controls, captions, data, proposals,
and all operational documents. Decision memos use Instrument Sans for titles, field
values, headings, and running copy.

- Preferred weights: 400, 500, 600, and 700.
- Use normal width for body and UI.
- A 95 to 97 percent width may be used for compact presentation labels.
- Do not use excessive geometric alternates that reduce readability.

### Geist Mono: evidence voice

Use Geist Mono for identifiers, dates, source lines, phase numbers, field labels,
status, page folios, and code-like evidence. It should feel like operating metadata,
not a typewriter theme.

- Preferred weights: 400, 500, and 600.
- Uppercase labels may use `0.08em` to `0.10em` tracking.
- Use tabular numerals where values align.
- Never use for paragraphs, memo body copy, or long quotations.

### Web type scale

| Display | `clamp(56px, 7vw, 96px)` | 0.96 | Newsreader 500 |
| H1 | `clamp(44px, 5vw, 72px)` | 1.00 | Newsreader 500 |

### Presentation type scale

- Cover headline: 38 to 48pt Newsreader 500.
- Section divider: 30 to 38pt Newsreader 500.
- Slide title: 24 to 30pt Newsreader 500 or Instrument Sans 600.
- Body: 17 to 21pt Instrument Sans 400.
- Metadata: 9 to 11pt Geist Mono 500.

### Memo type scale

- Document title: 20 to 24pt Instrument Sans 600.
- Summary heading: 10 to 11pt Instrument Sans 600 uppercase.
- Field values: 10.5 to 11.5pt Instrument Sans 500.
- Body: 10.5 to 11.5pt Instrument Sans 400 at 1.45 line height.
- Field labels and identifiers: 8.5 to 10pt Geist Mono 500 uppercase.

## 8. Layout and composition

### Grid

- Desktop: 12 columns, 24px gutters, 1240px maximum content width.
- Tablet: 8 columns, 20px gutters.
- Mobile: 4 columns, 16px gutters.
- Reading measure: 60 to 72 characters, maximum 720px.

### Composition principles

- Lead with one dominant idea, not a grid of equal cards.
- Use asymmetry with disciplined alignment.
- Align headlines, rules, metadata, and evidence to shared vertical axes.
- Leave large fields of negative space in light mode.
- Let dark mode carry denser color, not denser content.
- Use hairline rules to establish structure before adding containers.
- Prefer square corners. A 2px radius is allowed for interactive controls.
- Avoid shadows. Use contrast, borders, and spacing to establish hierarchy.

### Spacing

Use a 4px base with the following scale: 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96,
and 128px. Small gaps communicate relationships. Large gaps separate ideas.

## 9. Graphic language

### Rules and trajectories

Thin horizontal rules organize content. Diagonal trajectories derived from the V
may appear as faint construction lines or directional accents. Use no more than one
trajectory group per composition.

### Status grammar

- **Mineral:** active, in progress, linked, or primary.
- **Citron:** positive signal, recommended, validated, or next.
- **Coral:** decision, escalation, outgoing action, or attention.
- Always pair status color with a written label.

### Icons

Use simple line icons with square or butt terminals. Use 1.5px strokes at 24px.
Avoid filled circles, cartoon metaphors, and generic three-dimensional icons.

### Data visualization

Use Deep Ink or Pale for the baseline series, Mineral for the primary series, and
citron or coral only for a selected point, threshold, or decision. Label data
directly when possible. Avoid rainbow palettes and decorative charts.

## 10. Photography and imagery

### Founder imagery

Use real editorial photography with natural light, environmental context, and direct
but unforced presence. Show work, conversation, and decision-making. Avoid crossed-arm
executive portraits, artificial boardroom scenes, and generic laptop poses.

### Product imagery

Show real screens, work products, diagrams, documents, and operating artifacts.
Crop for the evidence that matters. Do not place every screen in a floating device
mockup.

### Abstract imagery

The graphic system is primarily flat and digital. If mineral references are used,
they should appear as macro structure, faceting, or material logic rather than literal
gemstones. Avoid blue-purple AI gradients, glowing brains, robot imagery, and neon
cyberpunk scenes.

## 11. Motion

Motion should communicate momentum and relationship.

- Micro feedback: 100ms.
- Hover and state changes: 160ms.
- Content transitions: 280ms.
- Major reveal: 480ms maximum.
- Enter easing: `cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1)`.
- Move easing: `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`.

The V mark may animate from one incoming stroke to three outgoing strokes once per
page load. Do not loop it. Respect reduced-motion preferences and remove decorative
movement entirely when requested by the operating system.

## 12. Artifact rules

### Website

Use light mode as the default. A first viewport may be light or dark, but the site
should remain predominantly light. Use dark sections for thesis statements, launches,
case-study transitions, or closing calls to action. The expressive mark appears only
on dark surfaces.

### Presentations

Use light content slides and dark section dividers. Every slide should have one
dominant statement, a short evidence set, and a mono folio or source line. Avoid
dense corporate templates with multiple equal boxes.

### Decision memos

Use Instrument Sans for all readable content. Restrict Geist Mono to field labels,
identifiers, dates, status, and source metadata. Use orange coral for the decision
heading and citron for a positive status. Default to a light page for printing.

### Proposals and reports

Use Newsreader for the cover and major section openers. Use Instrument Sans for the
body. Use Geist Mono for version, date, source, and status. Establish structure with
rules and spacing rather than shaded boxes.

### Outreach email

Use plain text or minimal HTML. Lead with a specific reason for writing. Keep the
message under 180 words unless context requires more. Use a simple Kevin Kokoszka /
Verata Ventures signature. Do not insert the full logo above every email.

### Social and campaign assets

Dark mode and the expressive mark are preferred. Use one short statement, one proof
point, and one clear source or action. Avoid quote-card templates and generic founder
advice graphics.

## 13. AI production contract

Every AI-created artifact must receive the compact brief in `brand/ai-brief.md` plus
an artifact-specific instruction. AI systems must use `brand/tokens.json` or
`brand/tokens.css` rather than estimating colors from an image.

### Required checks

1. The artifact communicates executive judgment and startup velocity together.
2. Light mode is the default unless the artifact is intentionally high-energy.
3. Newsreader is restricted to public editorial moments.
4. Instrument Sans carries body, UI, and operational documents.
5. Geist Mono carries metadata only.
6. Citron and coral are signals, not large decorative fields.
7. The founder-led structure is represented honestly.
8. Claims are specific and supportable.
9. Layout uses asymmetry, rules, and negative space instead of generic card grids.
10. Contrast and reduced-motion requirements are satisfied.

### Automatic rejection criteria

Reject and regenerate any artifact that introduces:

- purple or blue-purple gradients
- centered-everything layouts
- rounded cards throughout
- stock handshake or generic executive photography
- neon AI imagery
- serif memo body copy
- monospaced paragraphs
- coral or citron body text on light backgrounds
- fake team scale or unsupported claims
- a multicolor mark on a light background

## 14. Governance

This guide is the brand source of truth. `DESIGN.md` is the implementation contract,
and `brand/tokens.json` is the machine-readable token source. When they disagree,
resolve the conflict in this order:

1. explicit new founder decision
2. `BRAND.md`
3. `DESIGN.md`
4. `brand/tokens.json`
5. individual implementation

Record durable changes in the decision log and update the guide version. Do not
silently alter a core color, font role, logo construction, or audience posture in a
single artifact.
